Cleary opens Miami with a Weil leader, WilmerHale heads to Seattle

Tuesday, November 17, 2026

Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton poached Weil, Gotshal & Manges's Miami co-managing partner, a tax partner, to launch a new Miami office. Cleary also poached a Weil private equity partner in New York and a Weil appellate litigation partner in DC. Latham & Watkins rehired a Weil patent and trade secret litigation partner in Boston. Simpson Thacher & Bartlett poached an Orrick, Herrington & Sutcliffe partner in DC, a former Wisconsin chief deputy attorney general, who joins as co-head of Simpson Thacher's state attorneys general team. WilmerHale is launching a Seattle office, co-led by the former US Attorney for the Western District of Washington and a DC trial partner relocating to Seattle. Davis Polk & Wardwell poached a Cooley emerging companies and venture capital partner in Northern California. Holland & Knight poached a Balch & Bingham financial services partner in Jacksonville. Sheppard poached two Foley & Lardner sports industry partners, one in Dallas and one in Houston. Ballard Spahr added a fintech and payments lawyer from Womble Bond Dickinson in New Jersey as a partner. Davis Wright Tremaine is combining with Wargo French Singer, a 16-lawyer firm with offices in Atlanta and Miami, effective January 1. Bowman and Brooke is adding 18 lawyers from Ricci Tyrrell Johnson & Grey in Philadelphia, effective January 1.

On the business side, Bloomberg Law reports a federal judge ordered the White House to hand over the agreements it struck with nine law firms that pledged free legal services. The ruling came in a discovery dispute in the American Bar Association's lawsuit. Bloomberg Law reports Weil still has about $1 billion in client bills to collect, according to two people briefed on the wind-down plan. Davis Polk & Wardwell and Epstein Becker Green are providing legal counsel to Weil's wind-down committee. The American Lawyer reports Weil employees can keep working at the firm until Feb. 4, according to sources familiar with the timing. Latham & Watkins advised Diamondback Energy and Vinson & Elkins advised Double Eagle on Diamondback's roughly $5 billion purchase of Permian Basin assets.

Sources

Drawn from public reporting by The American Lawyer.
